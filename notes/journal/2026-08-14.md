**The regression.** My non-greedy citation regex fabricates a truncated prefix of a real id on
this repo's own corpus:
`backup` is a six-character mid-slug segment, so the lazy match stops there — the exact mirror image
of the greedy defect it replaced. Greedy fabricates on abutting six-char words; lazy on mid-slug
six-char segments; no regex can tell `-mjic4a` (real suffix) from `-driven` (abutting word)
syntactically. Round 2's proposed remedy was wrong; MY remedy — chosen after rejecting theirs
against the corpus — is also wrong, on the half of the corpus I did not check. And the comment I
shipped said "Verified against every real suffix shape in docs/decisions", which was false: I
verified the shapes I THOUGHT OF. Twelve ids, one loop, would have caught it in five seconds.

LESSON[claim-outruns-evidence]: "verified against X" is a claim about ALL of X. A verification that
enumerates from memory instead of from the corpus is the same defect as the cannot/never/always
comments — it just wears the word "verified".
Compiled response: partial — the corpus loop now exists as round 3's probe (committed inside
