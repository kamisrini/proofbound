# The Intent gate checks the unpushed range, explicitly (DEF-INTENT-TRAILER-BLOCK-001, round 2)

**ACCEPTED by the owner 2026-11-16** (scribe: the agent; acceptor: the owner, Law 9). Implements
[[BR-gate-integrity-4b2e08]] O-1. Continues [[CI-intent-trailer-block-xp2pke]], whose scope it
extends. It is a **new** intent rather than a revision because the canonical model cannot express a
change-intent revision: targets, `constrained_by`, and `supersedes` are validated as one list that
must ascend by key, and a `change_intent` supersedes reference always sorts before the
`requirement` targets every change intent must have.

Round 2 (`docs/verification/verdicts/intent-trailer-gate-review-round2-20261116.md`) found that the
gate checked only HEAD, which the commit-then-refresh-the-pin pattern defeats, and that its
`make check` coverage was accidental and silent. The owner chose the fix: the **unpushed range**.

## Allowed scope
- `scripts/intent-delivery-applicability.sh`: with no `--commit`, check every applicable commit in
  `upstream..HEAD` plus HEAD (HEAD alone without an upstream); with `--commit`, unchanged. Validate
  UTF-8 as strictly as the kernel, over the message, identity fields, and touched paths.
- `Makefile` and `docs/gates.md`: an explicit `make check` step running the gate over the real
  repository, registered as a blocking gate.
- `scripts/delivery-enforce.sh`: the gate path becomes an overridable seam (`PROOFBOUND_INTENT_GATE`)
  so `scripts/tests/delivery-enforce.test.sh` can prove the gate is invoked in order and that a
  failing gate stops enforcement; that self-test prints its captured output on failure.
- The shared corpus and both corpus tests, for the round-2 F1 boundaries.

## Explicit exclusions
A live `proofbound intent check` in `make check` or `delivery-enforce` (round 2 judged its omission
acceptable on its own). No kernel production code.

## Acceptance boundary
Calibrated mutation evidence on both sides; `make check` green; a non-author verdict (round 3).
