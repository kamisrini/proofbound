# VD-fix-discipline-0e0tnz: four rules for FIXING, derived from nine review rounds on one package

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Context:** `kernel/internal/store` took NINE adversarial review rounds over four days and roughly 6M verification tokens. Every round found real, reproduced defects; none came back empty. That is not a verification failure — the machinery worked — it is a FIX-QUALITY failure, and the evidence is that each remediation cycle introduced one to three new defects. Round 6 found a vacuous test written in round 5's fix; round 7 found a guard opened by round 6's fix; round 8 found a count left false by round 7's fix; round 9 found an unbounded overcount introduced by round 8's fix, a new hole opened by the same commit's scan fix, and a false cross-reference added by the commit that deleted three others. More review rounds cannot fix this: round 10 would find defects in round 9's fixes. The loop terminates only when fixes stop introducing defects. `internal/core` — same author, same ceremony, same week — took ONE round, which bounds how much of this is domain difficulty (store is genuinely harder: fork semantics, filesystem identity, a pool, crash safety) and how much is method (most of it).
**Decision:** Four rules, binding on any fix to a foundational package.
1. **The mutation proof comes BEFORE the fix commit, not after.** Break the code, watch the named test go red, then fix. Every defect that survived a round survived because the proof was written after the fix, when it could only confirm what was already believed. A fix commit names the mutation it kills.
2. **An equality invariant is tested in BOTH directions.** Round 9's HIGH existed because every assertion about `events_appended` checked `counted < added` and none checked `counted > added`; an equality was tested exclusively as an inequality, so an overcount was invisible by construction. Where a spec says "exactly", a single-direction test does not test it.
3. **No derived fact in prose.** Every number written into a document has gone stale — including the cell that declared itself the single home. A figure either carries the command that regenerates it, or carries its measurement conditions and date, or does not appear. This applies to counts, rates, durations, and SHAs equally.
4. **When a fix looks small and local, ask whether the DESIGN is wrong.** Twice the right answer was to delete the design, and both times it took two failed patch rounds to get there: the hand-rolled lock (→ `flock`, 208 lines deleted) and the append counter (→ the information is not recoverable; the INVARIANT was corrected instead). "Small and local" read as "cheap, just do it", when it was the signal to re-derive.
**Consequences:** Fixes get slower and fewer. Accepted deliberately: a fix that introduces a defect costs a whole review round (~250k-1.8M tokens) plus the remediation, which dwarfs the cost of proving it first. Rule 3 will feel pedantic and is the one most likely to be skipped under time pressure — it is also the one with the highest observed recurrence (a stale figure was found in four homes, a false claim survived five corrections). Rules 1 and 2 are candidates for mechanical enforcement and are advisory only until then; rule 4 is a judgement prompt and cannot be mechanised.
**Revisit when:** a foundational package completes with ONE verification round and no follow-on defects (the rules are working, consider relaxing); or a tenth-round-equivalent finds a defect introduced by a fix that followed all four (the rules are insufficient, and the next move is to change what a "fix" is allowed to be, not to add a fifth rule).
